How a course grade point is built

A course such as 3(2-3) has 2 theory credits and 1 lab credit (total credits minus the theory number). Each part is graded on its own scale and weighted by credits:
Course GP = (theory credits × theory GP + lab credits × lab GP) ÷ course credits.
Marks below 40 are F (0.00; credits stay in GPA until you pass the course). Marks of 40–59 are N: that part is withdrawn, left out of GPA and retaken in summer. Lab cut-offs are half: below 20 is F, 20–29 is N.
GPA = Σ(course GP × credits) ÷ Σ credits. CGPA = Σ(semester GPA × credits) ÷ Σ credits.

Rules & policies

From the Examination Regulations 2024 (Sukkur IBA) and the department’s new N-grade policy.

Minimum CGPA

You must keep a cumulative CGPA of at least 2.2. Below 2.0 you are dropped from the rolls. Between 2.0 and 2.19 you are placed on probation for one semester and must raise your CGPA to 2.2 in the next one, with no semester break. Two continuous probations are allowed if your CGPA improves. A third probation, or a second one that lowers your CGPA while it stays in 2.0–2.19, means you are dropped. If you have failed courses but your CGPA stays at 2.2 or above, you repeat or substitute them before the degree is awarded. GPA is computed every semester, summer included. Final-semester students are dropped only if CGPA falls below 2.0 and have two years to reach 2.2 for the degree.

N and F grades

N (theory 40–59, lab 20–29): only that part (theory or lab) is withdrawn. It is not counted in GPA or CGPA and you retake only that part in summer.
F (theory below 40, lab below 20): scores 0.00 and its credit hours stay in GPA. Once you pass the course, the F is no longer counted.
N or F in theory means retaking only the theory; in lab, only the lab. The same part of any course that needs it as a prerequisite stays blocked until you pass. A voluntary withdrawal still covers both parts (regulation 6.7).

Prerequisites

You cannot register for a course until its prerequisite is passed.

CourseNeeds

Summer, withdrawal and improvement

Semesters 1–6: no additional courses in Fall or Spring; up to 3 additional, failure and withdrawal courses in summer. Semesters 7–8 may add courses in regular semesters up to 21 credit hours in total. You may withdraw from one course per semester (two if your previous CGPA is 2.40 or below) within one month of classes starting. Only C+, C and C- grades can be improved, and the higher grade counts.

Final Year Project

FYP-I gets grade I (Incomplete) in semester 7 and is evaluated together with FYP-II in semester 8, so the calculator does not count it in semester 7.

FAQ

What do I need to graduate?At least 134 credit hours and every course passed and a CGPA of at least 2.2 (examination regulations). The degree takes a minimum of four years; HEC allows up to seven.
What is an N grade?N means you were not failed, but that part (theory or lab) is withdrawn and you retake only it in summer. It is left out of GPA and CGPA, grade points and credit hours.
What marks count as a fail?Below 40 in theory (below 20 in a lab) is an F and scores 0.00. Marks from 40 to 59 in theory (20 to 29 in a lab) give an N grade: it is not an F, you should improve the subject in summer, and that part is withdrawn and does not affect GPA.
Why are theory and lab entered separately?They are graded on different scales (100 and 50 marks) and carry different credit hours, so each gets its own grade before they are combined.
My semester credits are different from the scheme.On the calculator, leave out courses you did not take. For previous semesters, edit the credit hours next to each GPA.
Are Additional Math I and II counted?No. They are non-credit and are not part of the calculation.
Is this official?No. It is a student tool based on the published schema and grading plan. Your university’s result sheet is the final word.
